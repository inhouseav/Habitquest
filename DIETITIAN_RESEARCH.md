# Dietitian Survey — Research Summary

Source: `Dietician_Questions_for_Tobias_Project1-16.xlsx`, 16 anonymous dietitian
responses collected by Maddie before development (June 2026). Raw file lives
with Maddie — this is a distilled summary for ongoing app decisions, not a
replacement for the original.

## Format preference (16 responses)
- 6 chose "gamified app"
- 7 chose "healthy-habits book"
- 3 chose "kids cookbook"
This is exploratory feedback from a small, informal sample, not evidence the
app itself improves outcomes — don't claim dietitian endorsement anywhere in
the product.

## The one point every single dietitian agreed on
**Never frame anything around weight loss for children.** Multiple responses
explicitly warned this raises eating-disorder risk in growing kids. Several
also said not to answer "what's the average calorie intake" with a single
number — needs vary too much by age/activity/growth. This confirms the
app's existing rule ("never focus on body weight, never encourage dieting")
is correct and should stay non-negotiable in every module, including
Recipes and Healthy Snacks.

## What makes eating fun for kids (recurring across responses)
- **Involvement, not instruction** — help choose ingredients, shop, cook,
  plan meals, pick the recipe. This is the single most repeated theme.
- **"Eat the rainbow"** — colour-based variety challenges.
- **Growing their own food** — herbs, veggies, a garden pot.
- **Low-pressure exploration of new foods** — "food detective" (rate: smell/
  crunch/taste), a "would eat again / maybe / not today" scale, trying one
  new food a week, foods from other countries/cultures.
- **Family meals together**, DIY/build-your-own nights (tacos, pizza,
  wraps, rice bowls) — everyone assembles their own.
- **Fun presentation** — shapes, "vegetable faces," rainbow platters, dips.

## Snack suggestions (consolidated — already reflected in the app's "Hungry" idea picker)
Apple slices + peanut butter · veggie sticks + hummus · cheese + wholegrain
crackers · Greek yoghurt + berries · yoghurt + muesli · fruit + popcorn ·
cottage cheese + fruit · boiled egg + mandarin · cheese + cherry tomatoes ·
wholegrain toast + ricotta + tomato · nuts + fresh fruit · roasted chickpeas

yoghurt + berries · corn chips + guac/hummus + fruit · mini tuna sushi +

## Habits with the biggest impact (recurring)
Regular meals/snacks (not skipping, not grazing), a variety of whole foods
across the five food groups, daily movement, 8+ hours sleep, hydration
(mainly water), family meals together, strong relationships, routines,
reducing — not eliminating — discretionary/processed foods.

## Reducing screen time (recurring)
Don't just remove screens — replace with something genuinely enjoyable:
active outdoor play, sport, creative projects (drawing, Lego, crafts,
music), cooking/baking, board games, a "boredom jar" of activity ideas,
learning a new skill (instrument, coding, gardening). Several dietitians
explicitly said being bored sometimes is healthy and sparks creativity —
the goal isn't to eliminate boredom, just to have good alternatives ready.

## Implication for the Recipe Library
- Tag recipes by how much a child can be involved in making them (already
  sketched as "Cook together 👨‍👩‍👧" vs "Quick ⚡" in the sample page) —
  this is the single most dietitian-backed design choice available.
- Favour build-your-own formats (tacos, wraps, pizza, salad jars) where
  kids make choices rather than being served a fixed plate.
- Keep any "try something new" feature low-pressure — a rating scale
  ("would eat again / maybe / not today"), never a requirement to finish
  or like it.
- Never attach calorie counts, weight framing, or "reward for eating less"
  anywhere in Recipes, Snacks, or the star/reward economy.
